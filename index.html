      How this works

      Public data, matched and checked so the numbers hold up.

      Every night the build downloads the FAA Releasable Aircraft Database, with its current registrations, cancellations, reservations and aircraft models. An aircraft is included when its registrant is a tracked company or the FAA lists that company as its maker.

      • Own-design means the FAA lists the company as the maker, for example JOBY AERO INC and model JAS4-1. Chase planes, trainers and test conversions the company owns are counted separately.
      • N-numbers the FAA has assigned but not yet registered, such as Joby's N602HY, appear only under "All". Expired reservations are left out.
      • Companies that share a name with a tracked maker were removed by hand. These include the Archer Aviation LLC flight school, the Lift Aircraft LLC training fleet and AutoFlight LLC, which owns a business jet.
      • Aircraft held through a trustee appear when the FAA lists the maker. Supernal's aircraft, registered to TVPX Aircraft Solutions as trustee, are one example.
      • EHang, Volocopter, Eve, Vertical, Lilium and other foreign programs register aircraft with their home authority, so they show no U.S. registrations.
      • Flight activity comes from adsb.lol, which keeps a public daily history of every aircraft its volunteer receivers hear. A day counts as "seen" only when the aircraft reported airborne positions. The build checks the last ${seenDays} days.
      • The map draws each day's airborne legs, simplified to about 150 m (500 ft). It shows each company's own aircraft plus a short list of testbeds such as Joby's N101XW. Tracks stay on the map for 12 months, and featured flights stay permanently.
      • An aircraft shows up only while its transponder is on and a receiver is in range. Test aircraft often fly with ADS‑B off or under a privacy address, so missing days are normal.
      • Map imagery comes from Esri World Imagery (Esri, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics), shown in grayscale. The U.S. outline is from us-atlas, based on U.S. Census Bureau boundaries. Company status notes reflect public reporting as of September 2026.
      • The site is published from a nightly build. Its source code is private for now.

      All N-numbers

      N-numbers open the FAA record. ADS-B activity comes from adsb.lol's public history. Replay links open the aircraft's latest day on adsb.lol or ADS-B Exchange. Neither site hides aircraft at the owner's request, unlike FlightAware and Flightradar24.
